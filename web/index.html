How grades work

Built from official pro matches, Maps 1 and 2 only – the maps PrizePicks counts. Map 3 and FACEIT games never go into a projection.

Recent form counts most. A player's last few months weigh more than older matches, so a player in form (or in a slump) shows up quickly without one hot series taking over.

The maps matter. If the veto is out we use those two maps; if not, we predict them from both teams' recent vetoes and use how the player performs on each.

Match context. Betting markets tell us who is favoured – winning and losing teams put up different numbers. We also check the roster (stand-ins, new teams), how long since his last official match, and how big his sample is. Anything uncertain lowers the stake, not the honesty of the number.

Projection → chance → grade. The projection is compared with the number he actually needs (a 15.5 line needs 16), and turned into a win chance based on how much a player's totals normally swing from match to match.

Tested before it counts. Every factor was tested on 900+ past matches, using only information available before each one. If it didn't make grades more accurate, it doesn't move the grade – head-to-head, last 10, regional form and the match simulation are shown on the card for context.

What the grades mean (chance the pick wins): A+ 80%+ · A 74%+ · A− 68%+ · B+ 63%+ · B 57%+ · Lean under 57% (no units).
Units: A+ 2U · A 1.5U · A− 1U · B+ 0.5U · B 0.25U · Lean 0U. Thin markets, small samples, stand-ins and rust cap the units.

Grades are probabilities, not guarantees – a 60% pick still loses 4 times in 10. Not financial advice.
